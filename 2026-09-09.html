OpenAI says AI agents proved a Navier-Stokes singularity — but not the version the Millennium Prize requires

2026-09-08 research mathematics agents models

OpenAI said a swarm of roughly 10,000 autonomous agents, running on an unreleased next-generation model, produced an analytical proof and Lean formalization that fluid governed by the Navier-Stokes equations can develop a finite-time singularity — a spinning vortex that spirals inward and stretches without bound. The agents reached the result after about 88 hours and roughly 130 billion output tokens, with a separate model completing the machine-verified Lean formalization about 17 hours later. The claim comes with a significant caveat: the Clay Mathematics Institute's $1 million Millennium Prize criteria apply only to the unforced Navier-Stokes equations, while OpenAI's result covers the forced version — so it does not meet the prize's terms, and OpenAI says it isn't pursuing the award. The announcement also triggered a public credit dispute: NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster said OpenAI's Sébastien Bubeck learned of his and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge's related unpublished work (Lean-verified finite-time-blowup proofs for three other forced fluid systems, posted the same week) and pressured them over credit before OpenAI's announcement; Bubeck, OpenAI, and Sam Altman dispute that account and released messages they say show Bubeck offered to let Buckmaster's team publish first.

Why it matters

It's a striking demonstration of frontier AI tackling long-standing open mathematics problems at scale, but the forced-vs-unforced distinction and the credit fight are a reminder to read capability claims carefully — both what was actually proven and who did the underlying work.

Mistral raises €3B in Europe's largest-ever tech equity round, valuing it above €21B

2026-09-08 funding europe sovereign-ai infrastructure

French AI lab Mistral closed a €3 billion Series D at a post-money valuation above €21 billion, which the company says is the largest equity fundraising round ever completed by a European technology company. Samsung Electronics led the round, joined by co-leads Scaleup Europe Fund (managed by EQT) and existing investor PSG Equity, with Advent, BlackRock-managed funds, and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg joining as new investors. It's roughly double Mistral's €11.7 billion valuation from its €1.7 billion Series C a year earlier. CEO Arthur Mensch said the capital will go toward building and owning data centers in addition to renting outside compute capacity, framing the raise around European AI "sovereignty."

Why it matters

The round is a marker for how much capital European investors and governments are now willing to commit to keeping a frontier AI lab domestically owned, at a moment when compute buildouts are increasingly concentrated among a handful of well-capitalized US and Chinese players.

Cognition closes $2B round at $48B valuation, nearly doubling its May valuation

2026-09-08 funding coding-agents startups

The funding talks reported in mid-August have closed: Devin-maker Cognition raised a Series E of over $2 billion at a $48 billion valuation, led by new investors Andreessen Horowitz and Accel alongside existing backers Founders Fund, General Catalyst, and Avenir, with a broad syndicate including Nvidia also participating. That's nearly double its $26 billion valuation from a $1 billion round in May. Cognition said its annualized run-rate revenue has grown from about $492 million in May to nearly $900 million now.

Why it matters

A near-doubling of valuation in four months, on revenue that hasn't kept pace proportionally, underscores how aggressively investors are still pricing AI coding-agent startups even as the field gets more crowded.
