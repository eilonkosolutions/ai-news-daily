OpenAI launches GPT-5.6 model family and ChatGPT Work agent

2026-07-09 models agents enterprise frontier labs

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 in three tiers — Sol (flagship reasoning/agentic), Terra (everyday, GPT-5.5-competitive at roughly half the cost), and Luna (fastest, cheapest) — priced from $1/$6 to $5/$30 per million input/output tokens. Alongside it, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work, an agentic productivity tool built on GPT-5.6 that connects to apps like Google Drive, Slack, Salesforce, and GitHub to complete multi-step tasks and produce finished documents, spreadsheets, and presentations. New "max" and "ultra" reasoning modes trade additional compute for stronger results on demanding tasks, and OpenAI merged its standalone ChatGPT and Codex desktop apps into one.

Why it matters

The pairing of a cheaper, tiered model family with a workplace agent that reaches across third-party tools marks OpenAI's clearest push yet to move beyond chat into deployed, task-completing enterprise software — intensifying direct competition with Anthropic and Google on both fronts at once.

Anthropic expands Claude Cowork to mobile and web

2026-07-07 agents product enterprise

Anthropic took Claude Cowork, previously a desktop-only agent launched in January, to web and mobile for Max subscribers, letting tasks start on a laptop, run autonomously in the background, and be reviewed or continued from a phone. Anthropic also published usage data from 1.2 million anonymized Cowork sessions across more than 600,000 organizations: the largest use category (33.4%) was general business-process work such as compiling reports and reconciling spreadsheets, while software development accounted for only 8.7% of sessions. Doubled usage limits are running through August 5 to drive adoption.

Why it matters

The usage data and cross-device rollout underscore that coding-agent technology is being repurposed for general knowledge work far beyond software teams, and that Anthropic sees this broader office market — not just developers — as a primary growth lane.

News publishers ask court to sanction OpenAI over withheld evidence in copyright case

2026-07-09 policy legal copyright

The New York Times, the New York Daily News, and 15 other news organizations filed a motion in Manhattan federal court asking a judge to sanction OpenAI, alleging it misrepresented its ability to search ChatGPT logs and training data for copyrighted journalism. The publishers say an April deposition of an OpenAI engineer revealed the company had already run such searches despite telling the court they were infeasible, and that a court-ordered sample of 20 million chat logs was delivered with redactions so extensive it was unusable. They are seeking monetary penalties, attorney fees, and an adverse court finding on OpenAI's use of their content; OpenAI has denied the allegations.

Why it matters

A sanctions ruling could shape evidentiary standards across the wider wave of AI copyright litigation, and an adverse finding would materially strengthen publishers' position in one of the industry's highest-stakes legal fights over training data.
