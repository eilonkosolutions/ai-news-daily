OpenAI previews "Private Safety Processing," a safety layer compatible with zero data retention

2026-08-19

OpenAI announced it is testing Private Safety Processing with early enterprise and API customers, a system designed to flag misuse patterns across related interactions without giving OpenAI staff access to the underlying conversation content. The company says it will keep offering Zero Data Retention for frontier models — meaning prompts and responses aren't stored after a request completes — but argues that as models take on longer, more autonomous multi-step work, some serious risks only become visible across a chain of interactions rather than in any single one. When the new system's pattern-matching is triggered, it can send OpenAI a "narrowly defined signal" about a specific type of activity without exposing the raw content. OpenAI plans to begin rolling it out, along with a technical white paper, in September; it's aimed at eligible enterprise and API customers, not consumer ChatGPT plans. The move was widely read as a direct contrast with Anthropic, which requires data-retention logging for some of its safety monitoring.

Why it matters

It's a concrete attempt to resolve a real tension in agentic AI deployment — deep privacy guarantees versus the ability to catch abuse across long-running tasks — and stakes out a competitive position against Anthropic's more retention-dependent approach to safety monitoring.

Anthropic preparing supervoting shares for founders and a three-member governing trust ahead of its IPO

2026-08-18

Anthropic is preparing a dual-class share structure that would give CEO Dario Amodei and other co-founders a special class of stock carrying outsized voting power ahead of an IPO expected as soon as late September. Years of dilutive fundraising have left Amodei with roughly a 2% ownership stake, and founders' combined economic stake is reportedly under 5% — the supervoting shares would let them retain control despite that. Anthropic also plans to preserve its existing Long-Term Benefit Trust, a panel of non-shareholder trustees (including former Fed Chair Ben Bernanke) that can elect a majority of the board; the trust has recently narrowed to three active members. Separately, reports this week said Anthropic is arranging a revolving credit facility that could exceed $10 billion — more than four times the $2.5 billion facility it secured last year — to help fund its pre-IPO compute buildout. Under this structure, IPO investors would buy an economic stake with little practical influence over how the company is run.

Why it matters

As Anthropic heads toward one of the largest tech IPOs on record, it's moving to insulate leadership from the usual public-market pressures to cut costs or slow safety work — a governance bet that public investors will accept minimal control in exchange for exposure to frontier AI growth.

Meta ships a native Mac app for Meta AI, aimed at creators and small businesses

2026-08-19

Meta launched a dedicated desktop app for Meta AI on macOS, giving the assistant its first native home on Mac rather than requiring a browser tab. The 1.0-beta app is a 16MB download that runs on Apple silicon under macOS 15+, and adds two desktop-only capabilities: pointing the assistant at any on-screen window so it can read and discuss what's visible, and a system-wide dictation shortcut that drops transcribed text into whatever app is open. It also connects directly to a user's Instagram and Facebook professional accounts, Meta ad campaign data, and Google Workspace (docs, sheets, calendar, inbox), pitched squarely at creators and small businesses managing content and ad performance in one place. Per Meta's privacy policy, business data shared through the app can be used to train future models and target ads unless a user enables Incognito Mode.

Why it matters

It's Meta's clearest move yet to make Meta AI a daily work tool rather than just a chat feature bolted onto its apps, following rivals like ChatGPT and ClaudeAI onto the desktop and staking a claim with the creator/SMB segment specifically.
