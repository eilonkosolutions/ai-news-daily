AI selloff drags S&P 500 into correction as Nvidia sinks on report of SEC accounting probe

2026-11-20 markets nvidia regulation chips

The S&P 500 fell about 3.7% on Friday, Nov. 20, to 6,910.39, closing more than 10% below its Oct. 6 record and entering a correction, while the Nasdaq Composite dropped about 5.3% to 22,617.73, wiping out its gains for 2026, and the Dow lost 1.9% (outlets report slightly different percentages). Chipmakers led the decline: Nvidia fell roughly 12–14% after Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at the company, citing two people briefed on the matter; Reuters could not determine which practices are under review, the SEC declined to comment, and an inquiry is not a finding of wrongdoing. The report followed a Nov. 17 Wall Street Journal story that the SEC had opened a civil probe into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors, and capped Nvidia's worst week since 2008, down about 23% in the week after Jensen Huang's resignation. Micron fell nearly 14%, and Anthropic, in only its second day of trading, dropped about 9%. BNP Paribas described the move as "panic selling" as investors questioned whether AI enthusiasm has peaked.

Why it matters

The market's AI trade now faces a broad correction just as its bellwether, Nvidia, deals with leadership turmoil and regulatory scrutiny at the same time, raising the cost of capital for the industry's enormous compute build-out.
