US Government Pulls Fable 5 & Mythos 5 — Austria Asks EU to Host Anthropic

June 12 (suspension) · June 28 (Austria proposal) policy export controls governance Anthropic

On June 12, the US Commerce Department issued an export control directive ordering Anthropic to suspend all foreign-national access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5, its two top models, citing national security concerns. Both had launched only three days earlier, on June 9. Anthropic's understanding is that the government flagged a jailbreak that could unlock Mythos 5's cybersecurity capabilities; Anthropic disputes that it was a universal safeguard bypass rather than a narrow edge case. Unable to segment foreign nationals from US persons at scale, Anthropic disabled both models globally. Today, Austria's State Secretary for Digitalization wrote to the European Commission urging the EU to explore "the strategic establishment and participation of Anthropic within the European Union" — effectively asking the EU to bring Anthropic inside Europe's regulatory perimeter to insulate it from US access controls.

Why it matters

It's a landmark moment for AI governance — frontier models are now treated as national-security assets subject to export control — and it lays bare the tension between safety (limiting misuse of advanced cyber capabilities) and access (allied governments, businesses, and researchers abruptly cut off from tools they were paying for).

Zhipu GLM-5.2 Matches US Frontier Models at One-Fifth the Cost

June 26–28, 2026 models open-weight China cost

Beijing-based Zhipu AI (now branded Z.AI) released GLM-5.2 the day after Anthropic disabled Fable 5 — a 750-billion-parameter model with a 1-million-token context window running entirely on Chinese chips. On agentic benchmarks, GLM-5.2 lands within one percentage point of Claude Opus 4.8 at roughly one-fifth the API cost. Separate reports on June 28 indicate it matches Claude Mythos on benchmarks for finding software security vulnerabilities — the precise capability the US government cited when ordering Fable 5's suspension. GLM-5.2 is open-weight and export-control-immune, designed to run on domestic Chinese hardware.

Why it matters

It's a DeepSeek-style disruption at the frontier tier — an open-weight model reaching parity with the best US systems at a fraction of the cost, arriving one day after export controls walled off a comparable capability — suggesting such controls may accelerate competing supply chains rather than contain capabilities.

The End of "Tokenmaxxing": Companies Demand AI ROI

June 26, 2026 industry economics enterprise cost

CNBC reported a marked shift in enterprise AI spending: companies that spent the last two years pushing developers to "use as much AI as possible" are now imposing strict budgets and demanding measurable ROI. Lindy, an AI startup, switched 100% of its traffic from Claude to DeepSeek, saving millions annually — though its AI spend still exceeds its payroll. Uber blew through its entire annual AI budget in four months and has since introduced tiered spending caps. The shift is significant enough that analysts are questioning whether Anthropic's and OpenAI's current growth rates are their peak, even as both companies filed confidentially for IPOs in early June.

Why it matters

It marks a turn in the enterprise AI market from growth-at-any-cost toward measurable ROI — budgets, spending caps, and migrations to cheaper models — a shift large enough to raise questions about whether the leading labs' current growth rates are sustainable.

GLM-5.2 הוא בעל משקלים פתוחים וחסין לבקרות יצוא, ותוכנן לרוץ על חומרה סינית מקומית.
