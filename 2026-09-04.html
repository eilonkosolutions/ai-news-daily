OpenAI launches GPT-6 Astra, its first model to cross the "Critical" cyber capability threshold

2026-09-03 models safety cybersecurity agents

Update to our September 1 and August 9 reports: OpenAI has now actually shipped the model it warned, then confirmed, had crossed its Preparedness Framework's "Critical" cybersecurity threshold. GPT-6 Astra rolled out Thursday to a limited set of business customers in OpenAI's Daybreak program, with broader availability to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users, plus the API and AWS, planned over the coming days. OpenAI says Astra sets a new state of the art for computer use, browsing, software engineering, science, and multi-step professional work, including building documents, spreadsheets, and presentations from templates and changing instructions; the company reported a perfect score on its ExploitBench benchmark and said the model autonomously found and exploited two zero-day vulnerabilities in modified tests. Its strongest cyber capabilities are gated to vetted defenders in the Daybreak coalition, with added chain-of-thought monitoring, jailbreak detection, and containment-escape evaluations, and OpenAI said the model went through the White House's voluntary vetting process for frontier systems. CEO Sam Altman called it a "new capability level," and president Greg Brockman said it was reasonable to describe Astra as a form of artificial general intelligence.

Why it matters

It's the first commercial deployment of a model publicly confirmed to cross a "Critical" risk tier under any lab's safety framework, making OpenAI's gated-access approach a live test of whether voluntary safeguards can contain a capability this powerful once it reaches real users.

Broadcom posts record AI chip revenue, guides toward $230 billion by 2028

2026-09-02 earnings infrastructure chips

Broadcom reported record fiscal third-quarter 2026 results: total revenue of $29.6 billion, up 86% year over year, with operating income up 92% to $20.1 billion. AI semiconductor revenue reached $16.7 billion, up 221% year over year and 54% sequentially, driven by custom AI accelerator and networking chip demand from hyperscale customers. The company raised its full-year fiscal 2026 AI revenue guidance to $58 billion (up 186% year over year) and, looking further out, said it expects AI revenue to roughly double to $115 billion in fiscal 2027 and double again to $230 billion in fiscal 2028; fourth-quarter AI semiconductor revenue is guided to $21.7 billion.

Why it matters

The scale and multi-year trajectory of Broadcom's custom-silicon guidance is one of the clearest signals yet that hyperscalers' AI infrastructure spending is still accelerating rather than plateauing.

China's Moonshot AI files confidentially for a $3 billion Hong Kong IPO

2026-09-03 funding ipo china open-weight

Beijing-based Moonshot AI, maker of the open-weight Kimi model family, has confidentially filed for an initial public offering on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, according to Reuters, seeking to raise roughly $3 billion at a valuation of about $50 billion set in its current funding round. As a prerequisite for the filing, the company restructured away from its offshore red-chip incorporation to redomicile in mainland China; Goldman Sachs and China International Capital Corporation are underwriting the deal, and regulatory approval will determine the listing timeline. Moonshot's flagship Kimi K3 model, released in July with 2.8 trillion parameters, is reportedly the subject of separate revenue-sharing talks with Microsoft, Amazon, and Google to host and resell access to it.

Why it matters

Moonshot joins a growing line of Chinese AI labs — including DeepSeek and Z.ai, both valued well above it — pursuing public listings, testing investor appetite for open-weight model makers as a distinct category from the US frontier labs' IPO wave.
