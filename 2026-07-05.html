Unsealed court emails reveal Pentagon sought to override Anthropic's weapons red lines

2026-07-02 (unsealed), widely reported 2026-07-04 policy national security frontier labs AI safety

Court documents unsealed July 2 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California show a private email exchange between Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and Pentagon Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering Emil Michael. Anthropic maintains two hard limits on Claude's use — no fully autonomous weapons and no domestic mass surveillance — while the Pentagon's proposed contract language sought coverage for "all lawful uses." When Amodei said the language appeared to "completely remove" Anthropic's red lines, Michael reportedly did not dispute it and called the guardrails "just not workable." The presiding judge called the exchange hard to square with the government's simultaneous public framing of Anthropic as a national-security risk.

Why it matters

UN and ITU launch "AI for Good" Global Commission pairing tech CEOs with heads of state

2026-07-01 policy governance industry

The United Nations and the International Telecommunication Union launched the AI for Good Global Commission, co-chaired by Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff and Rwandan President Paul Kagame. Members include Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Amazon's Andy Jassy, Microsoft's Brad Smith, and Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark, making it one of the most senior gatherings of AI executives and heads of state under a UN mandate to date. The commission holds its first session in Geneva on July 8.

Why it matters

It formalizes a direct governance channel between the companies building frontier AI and national leaders, at a moment when countries are moving at very different speeds on AI regulation.

Meituan open-sources 1.6-trillion-parameter LongCat-2.0, trained entirely on Chinese chips

2026-06-30 models open-weight hardware geopolitics

Chinese delivery-and-tech firm Meituan released LongCat-2.0 under an MIT license — a 1.6-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model (about 48 billion active parameters per token) with a 1-million-token context window. The model was pre-trained and run entirely on a 50,000-chip cluster of domestic Ascend 910 accelerators, with no Nvidia or other US hardware involved, using in-house parallelism software. Meituan says it is the first model at this scale to complete both training and inference solely on domestic Chinese chips, and it has reportedly been topping usage rankings on the OpenRouter marketplace since release.

Why it matters

It's concrete evidence that Chinese labs can train frontier-scale models without restricted Western hardware, undercutting the assumption that US chip export controls limit how large or capable domestically-trained Chinese models can get.
