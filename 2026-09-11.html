OpenAI opens public beta of an Agents API, putting its Codex agent harness behind a single managed endpoint

2026-09-10 agents developer-tools models

OpenAI launched the Agents API in public beta, giving developers direct access to the same Codex harness that powers its coding agent: OpenAI manages sessions, orchestration, context compaction, and recovery, while developers supply their own tools and choose an execution environment — either OpenAI's managed sandboxes or their own infrastructure, with launch integrations from Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, and Oracle. Built-in features include automatic context compaction for long-running sessions, tool search, programmatic tool calling, and parallel subagents. There's no separate platform fee — usage is billed only for tokens and tools consumed — but during the beta, data residency is limited to the US and zero data retention isn't yet supported.

Why it matters

It packages the hardest parts of building a reliable long-running agent — session state, context management, recovery — into a managed API, lowering the bar for third parties to build production agents on OpenAI's own agent infrastructure rather than each rebuilding it themselves.

Oracle's cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubled last quarter as AI contract backlog hit $664 billion

2026-09-10 infrastructure earnings cloud

Oracle's fiscal Q1 2027 results (quarter ended August 31) beat analyst estimates: total revenue grew 30% year-over-year to $19.3 billion, cloud infrastructure revenue surged 121% to $7.4 billion, and total cloud revenue rose 62% to $11.6 billion. The company booked more than $30 billion in additional AI cloud contracts during the quarter, pushing remaining performance obligations to $664 billion — above the roughly $631 billion analysts expected. Oracle confirmed its $90 billion total-revenue target for fiscal 2027 and raised non-GAAP EPS guidance, even as capital spending of $28.5 billion pushed free cash flow to negative $5.4 billion. Shares jumped about 7% in after-hours trading.

Why it matters

The scale of Oracle's AI-driven cloud backlog — and its willingness to run negative free cash flow to build capacity for it — is a concrete data point on just how much compute frontier AI labs are contracting for, well beyond what quarterly revenue alone would suggest.
