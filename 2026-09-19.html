Anthropic names Accenture as its first embedded AI safety evaluator, with $2B pledged combined

2026-09-18 safety evaluation anthropic partnerships

Anthropic and Accenture announced a partnership to build a team of "embedded evaluators" — external reviewers given employee-level access inside Anthropic to observe model training, review deployment decisions, and speak directly with staff. The work will be led by Faculty, Accenture's specialist AI unit, and will include red-teaming models, running alignment assessments, and testing safeguards; Anthropic and Accenture each expect to invest at least $1 billion over the next five years, and Anthropic is funding Accenture's work directly. The partnership is non-exclusive — Anthropic says it's in talks with METR and other evaluators, with more partners to be named in the coming weeks. This is the first concrete step on Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" pledge earlier this month to give third-party evaluators permanent, employee-level system access.

Why it matters

It turns an abstract industry pledge — outside verification of AI labs' safety claims — into a funded, staffed program with a named partner, giving the first real test of whether "embedded evaluation" can work at the scale frontier labs operate at.

Consumers sue Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, and Google, alleging their AI-pacing pledges are illegal collusion

2026-09-18 policy antitrust legal pacing

Four individual plaintiffs filed a proposed class-action in the US District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging that Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, and Google violated Section 1 of the Sherman Act. The complaint centers on this month's public "pace the frontier" pledges — Dario Amodei's September 12 essay calling for industry-wide coordination to slow frontier model improvements, quickly endorsed by Sam Altman, Elon Musk, and Demis Hassabis — arguing that coordinated public commitments among direct competitors to slow product improvement amount to an unlawful restraint on output. The filing also points to OpenAI's own September 6 essay discussing slowdown coordination as an option, and a September 10 report that OpenAI had asked members of Congress whether an industry-wide slowdown could raise antitrust concerns.

Why it matters

It's the first legal test of the safety-driven coordination that saw the industry's biggest labs — and figures like Sam Altman and Elon Musk — publicly align within hours of each other this month; a plaintiffs' win could complicate the same kind of voluntary cross-lab coordination that regulators and even some lab leaders have called for on safety grounds.
