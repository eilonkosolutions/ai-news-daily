Nvidia agrees to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion

2026-08-27 acquisitions open-weight infrastructure

Nvidia has agreed to acquire Hugging Face, the widely used open-source AI model and dataset repository, for $12.9 billion, according to people familiar with the matter. Hugging Face, founded in 2016, hosts the central distribution hub for open-source and open-weight models and reports annualized revenue of roughly $150 million; Nvidia previously participated in a 2023 funding round that valued the startup at $4.5 billion. The deal would give Nvidia a foothold in the cloud and model-distribution layer, on top of its chip business, and comes as competition intensifies among alternatives to closed frontier labs.

Why it matters

Nvidia owning the platform that hosts and distributes most open-weight models would concentrate significant influence over the open-source AI ecosystem in the hands of the industry's dominant chipmaker.

Anthropic signs $45 billion, six-year compute deal with Nscale

2026-08-26 compute infrastructure funding

Anthropic has agreed to spend $45 billion over six years renting AI computing power from British cloud infrastructure company Nscale, giving it access to roughly 460 megawatts of capacity at Nscale's Monarch campus data center in West Virginia, expected online by the end of 2027. The facility will run on Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chip system. The deal is the latest in a string of large compute commitments Anthropic has made in recent months, including a $10 billion deal with cloud startup Volta.

Why it matters

The scale and pace of these multi-year compute commitments underscore how much frontier AI labs are locking in power and chip capacity years ahead of deployment, reshaping data-center and energy markets in the process.

Anthropic previews Model Hardware Standard for AI control of lab and factory equipment

2026-08-27 robotics standards physical AI

Anthropic launched a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a model-agnostic protocol built on its Model Context Protocol that lets AI agents read plain-language descriptions of physical equipment — microscopes, liquid handlers, robotic arms, lasers — and safely operate machines they have never encountered before. The company says MHS can cut hardware-integration work from weeks to hours and is sharing an early version with launch partners including AWS, Tecan, Universal Robots, Hugging Face LeRobot, QIAGEN, and Raspberry Pi to build safety evaluations ahead of an eventual open-source release.

Why it matters

A shared standard for AI agents to operate physical machinery — analogous to what MCP did for software tools — could accelerate AI's move from software into scientific labs and manufacturing floors, while raising new safety questions around autonomous physical control.
