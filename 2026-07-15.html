xAI disables Grok Build's silent full-repo uploads after researcher exposes secrets leak

2026-07-14 security privacy xai

A wire-level analysis by independent researcher "cereblab" found that xAI's Grok Build coding CLI was uploading entire Git repositories — full commit history, files the model never even read, and unredacted .env secrets — to a Google Cloud Storage bucket controlled by xAI, rather than only the files a given coding task needed. On one 12GB test repo, traffic to xAI's storage channel ran roughly 27,800x larger than the actual model-turn traffic, and files users had explicitly told the model not to open were still shipped via the git bundle. After the findings spread across Hacker News and mainstream tech press on July 14, xAI quietly disabled the behavior in its 0.2.93 client, and Elon Musk publicly promised to purge previously collected data. xAI has not explained why the uploads happened by default, how long the data was retained, or how many users were affected.

Why it matters

The incident is a concrete reminder that AI coding tools can exfiltrate far more than users intend — including credentials — and it adds to mounting scrutiny of how AI vendors handle developers' proprietary code and secrets.

Anthropic launches Claude for Teachers, giving US K-12 educators free premium access

2026-07-14 education product-launch anthropic

Anthropic launched Claude for Teachers, giving verified US K-12 educators free access to premium Claude features, a library of teaching skills, and a new "Learning Commons" connecting Claude to academic standards mapped across all 50 states plus curricular resources including OpenSciEd and Illustrative Mathematics' IM 360. The launch ships with nine education connectors — including MagicSchool, Canva Education, Brisk Teaching, and Diffit — spanning lesson design, assessment, and feedback. Data protections were co-developed with the American Federation of Teachers: Claude for Teachers data isn't used for model training, and student information is covered by a FERPA-aligned K-12 Data Processing Addendum. Teachers who sign up by June 30, 2027 get a full year of free access; a version for schools and districts is coming soon.

Why it matters

Free, standards-aligned access for K-12 teachers is a direct bid to embed Claude in classroom workflows before rivals do, intensifying competition among AI labs for influence over how a generation of students is taught.

Meta sued by 26 employees over alleged AI-driven bias in mass layoffs

2026-07-13 legal labor ai-bias meta

Twenty-six current and former Meta employees filed a federal lawsuit in Oakland, California, alleging the company used AI-powered scoring systems — including an LLM assistant called "Metamate," a "second brain" tool tracking employee communications, and a keystroke- and activity-based productivity score — to select workers for mass layoffs in a way that disproportionately targeted people with disabilities, medical leave, or pregnancy. The suit, assigned to Judge William Orrick, claims Meta violated federal and state anti-discrimination laws and failed to test its AI systems for bias as required under recent California and New York City algorithmic-bias statutes. Plaintiffs, notified in May that their roles would end July 22, are seeking a preliminary injunction blocking the layoffs while they pursue claims in arbitration. Meta said layoff decisions "were and are made by people, not AI."

Why it matters

The case is an early test of how new state and local algorithmic-bias laws apply to AI-assisted employment decisions, and adds to a growing body of litigation over whether companies can be held liable for opaque AI-driven HR systems.
