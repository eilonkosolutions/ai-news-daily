Google DeepMind ships Gemini Robotics 2 with whole-body humanoid control

2026-07-30 robotics deepmind gemini vla

Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2, a new family of vision-language-action models that extends control from a humanoid's upper body — the limit of the prior generation's table-top manipulation — to full whole-body motion, letting robots walk, crouch, stretch, and balance while manipulating objects in human-built spaces. The models can drive a five-fingered, 22-degree-of-freedom hand (demonstrated on the Apollo 2 humanoid) through fine tasks like tying knots and sealing a ziplock bag, and introduce multi-robot collaboration, where different robot types hand off subtasks through a shared semantic understanding. DeepMind says the models can also adapt to a new robot body within a few hours.

Why it matters

Whole-body control and cross-robot collaboration are widely seen as the next bottleneck for useful humanoid robots outside controlled factory settings, and a frontier AI lab pushing this directly signals robotics is becoming a core battleground alongside chat and coding models.

Nscale to acquire Anyscale for $1.65B, building a "full-stack AI hyperscaler"

2026-07-30 infrastructure m&a compute open-source

UK-based neocloud Nscale, which builds and powers its own data centers, agreed to acquire Anyscale — commercial steward of the open-source Ray distributed-computing framework — for roughly $1.65 billion, in a deal expected to close in the second half of 2026. Anyscale's roughly 200 staff across the US, Europe, and India move to Nscale, keeping the Anyscale brand and existing customers including Coinbase, Runway, and Bedrock Robotics; Ray itself stays under neutral governance at the PyTorch Foundation. Anyscale CEO Keerti Melkote framed the combined company as the industry's first "full-stack AI hyperscaler," pairing Nscale's GPU fleet and power infrastructure with Anyscale's workload-orchestration software.

Why it matters

It's a concrete step toward consolidation between the raw-compute (neocloud) layer and the ML-orchestration software layer, a pairing several infrastructure players are racing toward as customers push for one throat to choke across training, fine-tuning, and inference.

EU opens €30B call for seven AI "Gigafactory" sites

2026-07-30 policy infrastructure eu funding

The European Commission formally opened its call for tenders to build up to seven AI "Gigafactory" sites across the EU, backing the industry-led effort with up to €10 billion in EU and national funding and aiming to mobilize at least €20 billion more in private investment, for a total exceeding €30 billion. Each site is meant to host at least 100,000 AI chips — roughly quadruple current EU data-center scale — combining advanced processors, cloud and software systems, high-speed connectivity, and energy-efficient facilities for training and fine-tuning advanced models. Applications close November 12, 2026, with funding decisions expected in early 2027; the Commission has also signed letters of intent with AMD, Nvidia, and Qualcomm to secure hardware access for selected projects.

Why it matters

It's the EU's most concrete move yet to close its AI-compute gap with the US and China, betting public-private capital on domestic large-scale training infrastructure rather than continued reliance on foreign hyperscalers.
