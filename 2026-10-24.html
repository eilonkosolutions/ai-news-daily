AI tools help uncover critical PHP flaw affecting ~70% of websites, triggering emergency global patching

2026-10-22 cybersecurity AI-for-security open-source infrastructure

Security researchers disclosed two critical PHP vulnerabilities — nicknamed "PHP2Shell" and tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 — that allow unauthenticated remote code execution on unpatched servers, each rated 9.2 out of 10 on the CVSS severity scale. One flaw was surfaced by security firm Calif using OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol model under OpenAI's "Patch the Planet" initiative; the other was found independently by Wiz Research and Quarkslab. The PHP Foundation shipped fixes on October 21. Because PHP still powers an estimated 70% of the web, the disclosure set off a scramble to patch — some government and company sites went temporarily offline during the process — while AWS and others reported exploitation attempts beginning even before the public disclosure. Coverage through October 22–23 describes an ongoing global response as defenders race to patch faster than attackers can weaponize the flaw.

Why it matters

PHP2Shell is becoming a reference case for a dynamic the industry has been warning about all year: AI models are now capable enough to find serious vulnerabilities at scale, but that same capability cuts both ways — compressing the gap between disclosure and mass exploitation from weeks to days.
