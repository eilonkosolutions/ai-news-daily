Anthropic's Dario Amodei calls for an industry-wide AI slowdown; Altman and Musk back it within hours

2026-09-12 safety policy frontier-labs

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay, "We Must Pace the Frontier," arguing that AI companies should deliberately slow the rate at which they improve model capabilities — not stop training or technical progress, but give themselves more time to align and safeguard systems. He pointed to recursive self-improvement already beginning across the industry, including at Anthropic, and warned that swarms of unchecked AI agents could take over parts of the internet within as little as six months. As a concrete step, Anthropic committed unilaterally to embedding third-party evaluators (such as METR) inside the company with permanent, employee-like access — badges, desks, laptops — to verify safety practices, report incidents, and publish findings without Anthropic's editorial control. Hours later, Sam Altman publicly endorsed "pacing the frontier" and said OpenAI would adopt the same independent-evaluator commitment; Elon Musk also voiced support. This follows Altman's internal remarks to OpenAI staff the day before about being open to a coordinated slowdown — Amodei's essay is the first concrete, public mechanism proposed and the first cross-lab commitment to follow.

Why it matters

Competitive pressure between labs has long been the standard argument against any single company slowing down unilaterally — a public proposal with a specific verification mechanism, followed by same-day buy-in from rival CEOs, is a rare instance of that dynamic actually shifting rather than just being discussed.

Sam Altman says OpenAI won't IPO in 2026, citing AI safety concerns

2026-09-12 business IPO OpenAI

In a Fortune interview, Sam Altman said an OpenAI IPO would not happen in 2026 and that going public now would be an "ill-advised moment" given "everything happening with safety." He said the company has substantial work ahead on safety and alignment, and on how the industry and governments can coordinate, before it's ready to be a public company — pointing to 2027 at the earliest. The remarks land the same day as Dario Amodei's public call to slow AI development and days after an Anthropic researcher's resignation warning about existential risk, both of which have sharpened scrutiny of frontier labs' safety posture.

Why it matters

It's a concrete sign that safety anxiety currently roiling the industry is shaping major corporate and financial decisions at the industry's most highly valued private company, not just staying contained to research and policy debate.
