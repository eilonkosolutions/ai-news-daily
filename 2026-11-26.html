OpenAI preprint claims the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

2026-11-24 research math openai

OpenAI posted a preprint dated Nov. 24, "The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths," to its openai/math repository, claiming that the exponent ω governing how fast n×n matrices can be multiplied is exactly 9/4 over every field. The paper pairs an upper bound, extending OpenAI's October preprint that first reached 9/4 over the complex numbers, with a matching lower bound: formal arithmetic circuits would need at least about n^(9/4)/2 multiplication or division gates. If it holds, it would be the first proved lower bound above 2 and would settle a problem open since the 1960s, against the long-held expectation of many researchers that ω equals 2. The October upper bound has already received independent expositions from outside researchers, but the new lower bound has not been peer reviewed, and OpenAI's Lean formalization covers only a narrower case (circuits without division). The result is asymptotic and does not yield a practically faster algorithm.

Why it matters

A frontier lab claiming to resolve a famous open problem in theoretical computer science is the strongest sign yet that AI-driven mathematics research is producing headline results, and it puts pressure on the field to check machine-assisted proofs faster than they arrive.

Update: AI selloff briefly pushes the Nasdaq 100 toward a bear market before dip buyers step in

2026-11-25 markets chips tariffs

New since our last report: the AI selloff spread to Asia and hit Wall Street hard on Wednesday morning, Nov. 25, before largely reversing. In Asia, SoftBank Group fell 14.8%, Advantest 12.9% and Tokyo Electron 12.5%, while SK Hynix lost 11% and Samsung Electronics 9.9%, according to CNBC. In New York, the Nasdaq Composite dropped as much as 4.6% soon after the open, with Bloomberg reporting that the Nasdaq 100 briefly neared bear-market territory, but it closed down only about 0.6%. The S&P 500 slipped about 0.25% and the Dow rose about 0.3%. Nvidia ended down roughly 3–4% (reports differ), and Anthropic, which CNBC said had fallen below its $83 IPO price on Tuesday, swung from a near-10% drop to close up about 1%. Hotter-than-expected PCE inflation data and a new US proposal of Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners, including Taiwan, South Korea and Japan, added pressure, although reports say semiconductors are exempt from the proposed duties and hearings are set for January.

Why it matters

Violent intraday swings in the companies at the center of the AI build-out show investors are still deeply split on whether AI spending can be sustained and financed, and new trade measures aimed at the chip supply chain's home countries add another layer of risk.
