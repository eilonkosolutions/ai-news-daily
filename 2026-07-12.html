OpenAI ships GPT-5.6 model family and launches ChatGPT Work, an autonomous workplace agent

2026-07-09 models agents product-launch

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 as a three-tier family — Sol (flagship), Terra (balanced), and Luna (fast/low-cost) — priced at $5/$30, $2.50/$15, and $1/$6 per million input/output tokens respectively. The release adds an "ultra" setting that coordinates four agents in parallel on demanding tasks, and more predictable prompt caching with explicit cache breakpoints. Alongside it, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work, which merges the ChatGPT interface, the Codex coding tool, and a background automation agent into one desktop app that can take a goal, work across a user's connected apps and files for hours, and deliver finished documents, spreadsheets, and slides. It is rolling out first to Pro, Enterprise, and Edu plans, with Plus and Business to follow.

Why it matters

The pairing of a new frontier model with a full workplace-automation agent signals that major labs are now competing as much on end-to-end task completion as on raw model benchmarks — intensifying the race toward autonomous "AI coworkers."

Apple sues OpenAI, alleging coordinated theft of trade secrets by former employees

2026-07-10 litigation hardware industry-moves

Apple filed suit against OpenAI in federal court in Northern California, alleging that OpenAI's hardware chief Tang Tan — a former Apple VP — directed job candidates still employed at Apple to bring confidential prototype parts and documents to interviews, and that a former Apple engineer retained a company laptop containing technical files after joining OpenAI. Apple says the alleged information was used in developing OpenAI's own consumer hardware, including a proprietary metal-finishing technique. Apple states more than 400 former employees are now at OpenAI. The suit marks a sharp reversal for two companies that struck a high-profile partnership in 2024.

Why it matters

The case escalates competition for AI talent and hardware IP into open litigation between two of the industry's most influential companies, and could set precedent for how trade-secret law applies to cross-company hiring in AI.

SK Hynix's $26.5 billion Nasdaq debut underscores AI memory-chip demand

2026-07-10 hardware funding infrastructure

SK Hynix, the world's largest supplier of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) used in Nvidia's H100, H200, and Blackwell AI chips, listed American depositary shares on Nasdaq, raising roughly $26.5 billion — the second-largest share sale in history after SpaceX's recent IPO. Shares priced at $149 and closed the day up about 13%, with demand reportedly exceeding the US share allocation sevenfold. The company also confirmed a $4 billion advanced-packaging plant in Indiana alongside a much larger, roughly $390 billion fabrication buildout planned in South Korea.

Why it matters

The scale of investor demand for an HBM supplier — rather than a model developer or GPU maker — highlights how much of the current AI boom's capital is flowing into the physical memory and packaging supply chain underpinning AI data centers.
