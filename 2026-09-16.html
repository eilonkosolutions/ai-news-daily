Agility Robotics unveils Digit 5, its first humanoid built to work alongside people without safety cages

2026-09-15 robotics hardware safety industrial-ai

Agility Robotics unveiled Digit 5, a fifth-generation general-purpose humanoid it calls its first robot engineered for "cooperatively safe" work at industrial scale — able to operate near people without the physical barriers traditional factory and warehouse automation requires. A new leg design and cycloidal actuators give it 40% more payload (lifting up to 50 lbs repeatedly), while a new 90-minute-runtime battery recharges in 9 minutes, a 10:1 run-to-charge ratio (up from Digit 4's 2:1) that enables more than 20 hours of productive work per day. Its safety system uses AI and multiple sensors to continuously detect nearby people and autonomously steer around them, stop, or sit down depending on the situation. Agility expects early access in the first half of 2027 and general availability by end of 2027, expanding commercial deployment outside North America for the first time, starting with the EU and UK.

Why it matters

Removing physical safety cages is one of the biggest remaining barriers to humanoids working directly alongside people on factory and warehouse floors — a credible safety architecture, not just raw capability, is what turns humanoid robots from demo hardware into deployable industrial labor.

Google opens Claude Opus 5 access to all its engineers, breaking a long-standing internal Gemini-only rule

2026-09-15 industry coding-tools google anthropic

Google has updated its internal engineering policy to give all of its engineers quota-based access to Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 through its internal Antigravity development platform, according to Business Insider. Access to Claude was previously limited to select DeepMind teams and a handful of high-priority projects, while Google barred employees company-wide from using outside coding tools such as Anthropic's Claude Code or OpenAI's Codex, directing them to build with Gemini instead. Under the new policy, engineers select Opus 5 as a model option inside Antigravity rather than using Claude Code directly, and usage is capped by an individual quota; Google said "Gemini remains our primary and foundational model for internal development."

Why it matters

A company with its own frontier model formally opening the door to a rival's model for its own engineers is a rare admission that competitive pressure on coding-agent quality now outweighs the incentive to keep engineers inside a fully in-house stack.

Bernie Sanders and Steve Bannon share a stage at a "Pro-Human" AI summit in Washington, an unusual left-right coalition on AI risk

2026-09-15 policy regulation politics safety

Sen. Bernie Sanders and Steve Bannon appeared together at a day-long "pro-human" AI summit in Washington organized by the Future of Life Institute, drawing roughly 300 attendees along with speakers including actors Joseph Gordon-Levitt and Ashley Judd, AFL-CIO president Liz Shuler, and AFT president Randi Weingarten. Sanders told the crowd he and Rep. Despite appearing on the same stage, Sanders and Bannon showed little agreement on how AI should actually be regulated.

Why it matters

A progressive senator and a MAGA strategist finding common cause on AI risk — even while disagreeing on the remedy — signals that anxiety about frontier AI is starting to scramble conventional political alliances rather than sorting along the usual left-right lines.
