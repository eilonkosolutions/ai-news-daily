Apple releases iOS 27 with its biggest Siri overhaul since 2011

2026-09-14 products apple assistants on-device-ai

Apple shipped iOS 27 on September 14 to all supported iPhones (iPhone 11 and later, iPhone SE 2nd-gen and later), headlined by "Siri AI" — Apple's most significant rework of its voice assistant since Siri launched in 2011. The new Siri combines personal context, on-screen awareness, and general web knowledge to hold more natural conversations, give richer answers, take actions inside apps, and carry chat history across devices. It's a staggered rollout, not a full launch: Siri AI is opt-in and beta, gated behind a waitlist, available in English only, requires an iPhone 15 Pro or newer (including iPhone 16/17, Air, and Duo models), and is blocked entirely for users in the EU and China.

Why it matters

After years of public delays to its generative-AI-powered Siri rebuild, Apple is finally shipping — but a beta, waitlist-gated, hardware-restricted, English-only rollout excluding two major regulatory markets shows how much caution the company is still applying to its most consequential AI product.

Workflow-orchestration startup Temporal raises $550M at a $12.55B valuation as agentic AI drives demand

2026-09-14 funding infrastructure agents

Temporal, whose open-source workflow engine is widely used to manage long-running and agentic AI processes, closed a $550 million Series E at a $12.55 billion valuation — more than double the $5 billion it was valued at in February. Lightspeed Venture Partners led the round, joined by co-leads Wellington Management, Goldman Sachs Alternatives' Growth Equity arm, and Tiger Global, plus new investor T. Rowe Price and existing backers including Andreessen Horowitz, Sequoia Capital, and Index Ventures. The company said its annualized revenue run rate has passed $250 million (up more than 200% year over year), open-source installations surpassed 43 million in August (up 134% since January), and it now counts more than 4,300 paying customers, including OpenAI, Nvidia, Snap, Netflix, and JPMorgan Chase.

Why it matters

As companies move from single AI model calls to long-running, multi-step agents, the infrastructure layer that keeps those agent workflows running reliably is becoming its own large and fast-growing market — this round is one of the clearest funding signals yet of that shift.
