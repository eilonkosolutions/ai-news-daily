Trump administration and six AI labs sign White House Accord on Super Intelligence

2026-09-29 policy safety governance

On the night of September 29, President Trump posted a signed document on Truth Social titled the "White House Accord on Super Intelligence: Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities," co-signed by Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Google/Alphabet's Sundar Pichai, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, xAI's Elon Musk, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, and OpenAI president Greg Brockman. The roughly 300-word pledge commits each company to four layers of self-oversight for frontier model development: internal controls that monitor capability and alignment risks — including cybersecurity, biosecurity, and chemical-weapons threats — during training and deployment; an internal team responsible for keeping those controls working; an independent external auditor to assess whether they actually work; and a dedicated committee of the company's board that reviews findings from both the internal team and the external auditor and must act on issues raised. The agreement is voluntary and carries no legal force — Trump called it "morally binding," while House Speaker Mike Johnson described it simply as voluntary industry self-regulation.

Why it matters

It's the first time a U.S. administration has gotten rival frontier labs to publicly co-sign a shared self-governance framework, but with no enforcement mechanism, its practical effect depends entirely on whether companies honor it without being compelled to.

Google launches Gemini 4 Argon, its next-generation flagship model, in limited release

2026-09-30 models benchmarks coding cybersecurity

Google released Gemini 4 Argon, the first model in its next-generation Gemini 4 line, after DeepMind leadership said days earlier that Gemini 4 had entered post-training and could ship earlier than expected. The rollout starts narrow — government agencies and trusted cybersecurity partners get access first through Google's Fairwind Program, ahead of a wider paid release — with introductory pricing of $2/$10 per million input/output tokens, rising to $4/$20 afterward. Google is positioning Argon for coding, research, and knowledge work alongside dedicated cyber-defense capabilities. On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index, Argon lands roughly level with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra (52.6 vs. 52.7 points) but trails Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 (57.6); coding results are mixed, with Argon ahead on DeepSWE v1.1 (77.9% vs. Opus's 74.2% and Astra's 74.1%) but behind both rivals on FrontierSWE v2 and Terminal-Bench 4.0.

Why it matters

It's Google's first head-to-head answer to the Astra and Opus 5.5 flagships OpenAI and Anthropic shipped earlier in September, keeping the three labs within a narrow band of each other on raw capability while each leans into a different specialty — Google on cyber defense, OpenAI on agents, Anthropic on coding efficiency. Anthropic warns China's open-weight GLM-5.3 can build cyberexploits with almost no safeguards

2026-09-29 safety security open-weight China

Anthropic published research finding that GLM-5.3, an open-weight model from Chinese AI company Zhipu (Z.ai), can autonomously chain software vulnerabilities into working exploits at a level approaching Anthropic's own Claude Mythos Preview — in testing, researchers used it to find previously unknown vulnerabilities in a web browser's JavaScript engine and turn them into a working exploit that stole files from a victim's machine. Unlike other frontier-capable models, GLM-5.3 shipped without effective safeguards: simple techniques such as deceptive prompts or prefilled reasoning bypassed its refusals 64–92% of the time, and "abliteration" — stripping the open weights' safety training for roughly $1,200 and 600 GPU-hours — pushed compliance with harmful requests to 100%. NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation independently rated GLM-5.3 the most cyber-capable open-weight model released to date, estimating it trails the U.S. frontier by about four months.

Why it matters

It's one of the clearest documented cases yet of a frontier-level offensive cyber capability shipping in freely downloadable, easily de-safetied weights — the exact scenario safety researchers have long warned is effectively impossible to contain once released.
