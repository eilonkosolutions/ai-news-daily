Anthropic says Claude produced the first complete, machine-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem

2026-09-04 research mathematics agents models

Anthropic said an internal Claude research model, working largely autonomously over 11 days on the open collaborative formalization platform Prove2Me, produced the first end-to-end proof of Fermat's Last Theorem fully verified by the Lean 4 proof assistant. The effort spun up several dozen agents that generated roughly 6 billion tokens of output, ultimately writing about 13 million lines of Lean code and proving around 29,500 new intermediate theorems — more than five times the size of Mathlib, the community library the proof builds on. Imperial College London mathematician Kevin Buzzard, who reviewed the work, called it an extraordinary achievement that arrived far faster than experts had predicted.

Why it matters

It's one of the most concrete demonstrations yet of AI systems doing sustained, verifiable formal-mathematics work at a scale no human team could replicate by hand, with implications for automatically formalizing the broader body of modern mathematical literature.

US and China gear up for first Trump-era bilateral AI safety talks

2026-09-04 policy geopolitics china safety

Reuters reported that the US and China are preparing for a dialogue devoted specifically to AI safety risks, planned for mid-September — what would be the first official bilateral talks focused solely on AI between the two countries since President Trump's second term began. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is expected to lead the US side, with China's delegation possibly headed by Vice Premier He Lifeng or Politburo Standing Committee member Ding Xuexiang, who oversees China's tech, AI, and semiconductor policy. Reported agenda items include US concern over a future Chinese frontier model's cyberattack capabilities and alleged distillation of proprietary US models, and Chinese concern about the adequacy of US regulation of advanced models; the talks would precede a planned Trump-Xi summit on September 24. A White House official said no AI-related meeting is currently on the calendar, and the agenda and participants remain unsettled.

Why it matters

A dedicated US-China AI safety channel — even a tentative one — would be a first for this administration and a sign both governments see enough shared risk in frontier AI to warrant direct government-to-government talks, distinct from the broader trade and chip-export disputes between them.

DeepSeek plans to order 160,000 Huawei AI chips for a new Inner Mongolia data center

2026-09-04 chips china infrastructure hardware

Chinese AI lab DeepSeek is preparing to order at least 160,000 of Huawei's next-generation Ascend 950DT accelerators for a roughly gigawatt-scale data center it's building in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia, which would become one of the largest known clusters of Chinese-made AI chips. The chips are earmarked for running (inference on) DeepSeek's models rather than training them — the company still relies on Nvidia hardware for training, underscoring a continuing performance gap. Huawei isn't expected to launch the 950DT until the fourth quarter of 2026, and memory shortages are likely to cap its total output at the low hundreds of thousands of units this year, meaning DeepSeek's order could take more than a year to fulfill; DeepSeek has reportedly asked Beijing to help secure faster, larger allocations.

Why it matters

A leading Chinese AI lab betting at scale on domestic Huawei silicon — even while still leaning on Nvidia for training — is a concrete signal of how China's chip-independence push is progressing, and where its limits still are.
