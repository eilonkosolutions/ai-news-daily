Anthropic hands its Agent Skills standard to the Agentic AI Foundation

2026-10-22 open-source agents standards Anthropic

Anthropic donated Agent Skills — the open standard for packaging specialized knowledge and workflows for AI agents, built around a SKILL.md file plus supporting scripts and reference material — to the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), a Linux Foundation project Anthropic co-founded with Block and OpenAI in 2025. The donation covers the standard itself plus the Agent Skills name and logo, its GitHub organization, and the agentskills.io domain, mirroring Anthropic's earlier donation of the Model Context Protocol to the same foundation. Anthropic says more than 70 agent platforms — including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, and VS Code — have integrated Agent Skills since it published the standard in December 2025, with roughly 1 million skills published and nearly 280 million installed. Engineers from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, and other companies will now maintain the standard as individuals under AAIF, with a roadmap covering versioned releases, a formal proposal process, and security guidance for skill authors and users. The move came alongside three other open-source AI tools being added to AAIF the same day, from OpenAI, Hugging Face, and Alibaba.

Why it matters

Moving a widely-adopted agent-tooling standard from a single vendor to neutral foundation governance — the second such handoff after MCP — signals that interoperable, vendor-neutral infrastructure is becoming the norm for how AI agents are built and extended across the industry.

DeepSeek raises roughly $15 billion led by CATL and Tencent, days after US restrictions

2026-10-22 funding China DeepSeek AI infrastructure

DeepSeek is wrapping up a roughly 100-billion-yuan (about $15 billion) funding round, with battery maker CATL and Tencent among the largest backers, according to Bloomberg; Midea, Meituan, Geely, and GigaDevice reportedly joined as new investors. The fundraising target reportedly grew during negotiations, from an initial 50 billion yuan to 80 billion yuan before settling near 100 billion yuan; some terms and the final investor list were still described as subject to change as of October 22. The round follows reports that DeepSeek has hired CITIC Securities to work toward a Shanghai STAR Market listing, with a filing possible as early as late 2026 and a listing targeted for 2027. The timing is notable: it lands just days after the US Department of Homeland Security's order barring DeepSeek products from federal civilian agencies and contracts became public (first published in the Federal Register October 19–20), citing national-security concerns over data transfer to China and potential malware risk.

Why it matters

A major Chinese domestic capital infusion arriving right after a US federal procurement ban underscores how DeepSeek's path forward increasingly runs through China's own capital markets and industrial giants rather than through access it's being cut off from in the US.
