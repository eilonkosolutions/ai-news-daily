US blacklists Alibaba's Singapore unit and Nvidia customer Megaspeed over AI chip diversion risk

2026-11-23 export controls chips china policy

The US Commerce Department added seven Southeast Asian companies to its Entity List on Monday, Nov. 23, including Apex Enterprise Solutions, a Singapore subsidiary of Alibaba, and several affiliates of Megaspeed, a compute-rental firm that runs Nvidia chips. The list covers Megaspeed International, Apex and Swiftdata in Singapore; Megaspeed (Thailand); and MSFusion, PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix in Malaysia, citing a "significant risk" of diverting advanced computing chips to unauthorized end users; the rule is due for Federal Register publication on Nov. 25. Reuters reported that Apex had paid about $4.2 billion to Megaspeed and others as prepayment for computing capacity in Southeast Asia. Alibaba called the listing "erroneous," said Apex has never diverted export-controlled chips, and said it will pursue legal options; Megaspeed has previously said it complies with US export controls. Nvidia itself was not listed, though the action follows a probe running since 2024 and lands amid scrutiny of Jensen Huang's ties to a former Megaspeed executive.

Why it matters

Blacklisting a unit of China's largest cloud provider shows Washington now targeting the rented-compute arrangements in Southeast Asia that Chinese firms have used to reach Nvidia hardware, on top of last week's licensing rule for the region.

Chip stocks give back much of the Huang rally; AMD falls 9% after registering 337 million shares tied to OpenAI and Meta warrants

2026-11-24 markets chips amd nvidia

A day after Nvidia's 31% surge on Jensen Huang's reinstatement, semiconductor stocks reversed sharply on Tuesday, Nov. 24: Nvidia closed down about 5.9% at $155.38, still roughly 35% below its Oct. 6 record, while Micron fell about 11% on worries about slowing high-bandwidth-memory demand, Intel about 8% and the iShares semiconductor ETF (SOXX) about 8%. AMD fell about 8.9% after filing an automatic shelf registration late on Nov. 23 covering roughly 337 million shares for resale by existing holders, including 160 million shares each under warrants held by OpenAI and Meta. AMD will not sell shares or receive proceeds, and neither warrant is currently exercisable — they vest as the two companies deploy 1 to 6 gigawatts of AMD Instinct GPUs, with the final tranche requiring a $600 share price — but the filing put the potential dilution of roughly a fifth of AMD's share count in front of investors. The Nasdaq fell 1.5% and the S&P 500 0.5%, while the Dow rose 0.85% to close above 50,000 as money rotated out of AI names.

Why it matters

The quick reversal shows the AI trade remains fragile after last week's correction, and the AMD filing is a reminder that the equity-for-compute deals AI labs struck with chipmakers carry real dilution costs for shareholders.
