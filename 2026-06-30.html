OpenAI Previews GPT-5.6 Family Under Government-Gated Access

June 26, 2026 models frontier AI government review cybersecurity

OpenAI began a limited preview of its GPT-5.6 family — Sol (flagship), Terra (balanced mid-tier), and Luna (fast/cheap) — priced at $5/$30, $2.50/$15, and $1/$6 per million input/output tokens respectively. Under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, all three are classified as High capability in cybersecurity and in biological/chemical risk; on ExploitBench, Sol matches Anthropic's Mythos Preview using roughly a third of the output tokens. At the U.S. government's request, the models are initially limited to about 20 trusted partner organizations rather than a general release, with OpenAI saying broader availability will follow "in the coming weeks."

Why it matters

It's the clearest sign yet that government review has moved to the center of how frontier models ship — a major lab voluntarily gating a flagship release behind federal sign-off before the public ever sees it.

US Government Partially Restores Anthropic's Mythos 5 After Export-Control Standoff

June 26, 2026 policy export controls Anthropic national security

The Trump administration granted Anthropic permission to redeploy its Mythos 5 model to roughly 100 vetted U.S. companies and federal agencies, partially reversing the export-control directive that forced Anthropic to disable Mythos 5 and Fable 5 globally on June 12. The restoration is narrow — limited to critical-infrastructure defenders and government-cleared partners — and Anthropic's separate federal lawsuits over the Defense Department's "supply chain risk" designation, filed in March, remain active on a different legal track.

Why it matters

It marks a partial thaw in the first major case of a frontier AI model being treated as an export-controlled national-security asset, but the underlying legal dispute between an AI lab and the Pentagon is still unresolved.

Anthropic Launches Claude Science, an AI Workbench for Researchers

June 30, 2026 products science Anthropic research tools

Anthropic released Claude Science in beta for Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise users on macOS and Linux, unveiled at a livestreamed "AI for Science" event. The app bundles more than 60 scientific databases (including UniProt, PDB, and ChEMBL) into a single environment, natively renders outputs like 3D protein structures and genome browser tracks, and keeps every result traceable to its underlying code for auditability. Anthropic is also funding up to 50 external research projects with up to $30,000 in compute credits each, with applications open through July 15.

Why it matters

It's a concrete bet that domain-specific, auditable AI workbenches — not general chat assistants — are the next layer of value for scientific research, and a sign of AI labs diversifying revenue beyond general-purpose subscriptions.
